## 1. Logos / Faithlife AI: how answers are grounded in the user's licensed library, how sources are cited, and what principles Faithlife has published

### Takeaway
Logos is the most mature "closed-corpus" design in this field. Smart Search, Smart Synopsis and the conversational Study Assistant (Logos 46, Nov 2025) retrieve from the user's licensed library instead of the open web. They footnote each sentence to a book and page, and the stated commitments are no open web, page-level citations, no flattery and refusing to ghostwrite. Faithlife has published convictions and talks rather than one formal "AI principles" document. One independent reviewer noted that answers are short and often paraphrase instead of quoting.

### Cited Findings
- **[Vendor, undated]** Logos says "the majority" of its AI tools draw only from the user's Logos library, not the open internet. It describes its AI as "illuminating the highest-quality biblical texts, datasets, and books, giving you deeper insights in less time." — [Logos Help Center: How Logos uses AI](https://support.logos.com/hc/en-us/articles/35181728416397-How-Logos-uses-AI)
- **[Vendor, undated]** Smart Search is a natural-language, meaning-based search that also matches exact words. A search for "glossolalia" also finds articles on "speaking in tongues." - **[Vendor, undated]** "Smart Synopsis uses cutting-edge AI to distill the top five search results into a short, easy-to-digest paragraph." Each sentence has a footnote to the book and page used, and the synopsis can set out differing views on a topic in one summary. — [Logos Help: Smart Search](https://support.logos.com/hc/en-us/articles/23526184005261-Smart-Search)

### Inferences
- Logos's pattern is the closest prior art for Margin's "grounded in commentaries with verified quotes": closed corpus, retrieval before generation, sentence-level footnotes to book and page, and a bibliography with deep links that open the source.
- Logos's own reviewer flagged paraphrase versus quotation. Margin's "verified quotes" promise should keep exact, checked quotations visibly separate from AI paraphrase.
- In 2024 the Sermon Assistant generated outlines, but the podcast describes a hard-coded refusal to write sermons. Either Logos tightened its policy over time or the refusal applies only to whole drafts. Either way, the reputational line in this market sits at ghostwriting for preachers, not at study help.
- The "library vs. wider catalog" nuance matters. Answers drawn from resources the user hasn't licensed blur the grounding story, and Margin should say plainly which corpus each answer came from.

### Gaps
- No Logos statement was found on which LLM vendors it uses, whether prompts or notes train models, or how long data is kept. The direct fetch of "How Logos uses AI" was blocked, so check it.
- No finished Faithlife "AI principles" statement was found. The Gons statement was described as in progress.
- Exact launch dates for Smart Search and Smart Synopsis were not confirmed.

## 2. Magisterium AI (Longbeard): grounding in magisterial documents, citations, evaluations, known errors, and related Catholic AI efforts

### Takeaway
Magisterium AI (launched 2023) retrieves from a curated Catholic corpus and footnotes its answers. The corpus grew from hundreds of documents in 2023 to a claimed 23,000+ magisterial documents plus about 2,300 scholarly works. Independent tests show that footnotes do not guarantee faithful synthesis. Problems documented by reviewers:
- it stated contested positions as fact (women deacons, RNS, Nov 2025);
- it mixed magisterial texts with journal opinion;
- it misdescribed historical figures (Augustine, Aquinas);
- it could be steered by rephrasing.

Longbeard now sits at the centre of a wider Catholic AI push (Builders AI Forum, Hallow integration) that runs alongside Vatican teaching on AI (*Antiqua et Nova*, Jan 2025; *Magnifica humanitas*, May 2026).

- **[Secondary, 2023]** Early coverage says it was trained on "a database of 456 Church documents," including Scripture, the Catechism of the Catholic Church and the Code of Canon Law. - **[Church doc, signed May 15, 2026; published May 25, 2026]** Pope Leo XIV's first encyclical, *Magnifica humanitas*, is on "preserving the human person in the age of artificial intelligence."

### Inferences
- Magisterium AI and Logos share a pattern: closed authoritative corpus, retrieval and footnotes. The documented failures happen at the synthesis step: overstating, closing open questions and leaning on secondary opinion. Footnotes help users check answers but do not stop the model misreading what it cites.
- Mixing authority tiers caused trust damage: the Magisterium label alongside journal opinion. Margin should keep Scripture, a named commentator's verbatim words and the AI's synthesis visually and verbally distinct.
- Shadle's finding that it misdescribed Augustine and Aquinas is a direct warning for Margin's "in character" commentator mode. Answering as a historical figure is exactly where a model can attribute views the person never held.
- Consistent wrong answers across three tries show that fixing randomness does not fix correctness. Margin's evaluations need expert review, not just consistency checks.
- Vendor claims of "cannot hallucinate" conflict with partners' own disclaimers (Hallow). Margin should not make no-hallucination claims.

### Gaps
- No independent quantitative accuracy evaluation of Magisterium AI was found. No America Magazine review was found.
- The underlying model provider is not disclosed in the sources found. A critic's claim that it licenses a commercial LLM API without clear disclosure could not be verified.
- The Pillar's "Theology and the machine" piece was not retrieved. — [The Pillar](https://pillarcatholic.com/p/theology-and-the-machine)
- No search extract showed whether *Magnifica humanitas* addresses religious chatbots or persona apps specifically.

## 3. Other notable tools: what they actually do and how reputable they are (Bible Chat, YouVersion, Gloo, BibleProject, Accordance/Olive Tree, Bible.ai/BibleGPT, persona apps, Hallow, Pray.com, sermon tools)

### Takeaway
The market splits three ways:
- **Library-grounded study tools** (Logos, Magisterium AI, Hallow AI) cite closed corpora.
- **Mass-market general-LLM wrappers** (Bible Chat, Creed, Text With Jesus) are popular and revenue-driven, with weaker grounding.
- **Infrastructure and benchmark players** (Gloo).

The most reputable large platform, YouVersion, has deliberately not launched a public theological chatbot because of accuracy. Accordance and Olive Tree appear to have no built-in generative AI. No BibleProject AI statement was found.

## 4. Persona controversies: AI clergy, saints and Jesus personas

### Takeaway
Persona failures share three features: the bot claimed or implied real clerical or divine identity; users jailbroke it into sacramental or pastoral acts; and the persona lent unearned authority to wrong answers. Catholic Answers' "Father Justin" (Apr 2024) was the defining case. It was "laicized" within days into a lay "Justin." Church documents and at least one Bible publisher's licence (Biblica, 2026) now push toward:
- explicit AI disclosure;
- never presenting AI as God, Christ or a pastor or counselor;
- caution about simulated relationships.

### Inferences
- What went wrong with Father Justin, in sequence:
  1. A clerical costume implied sacramental authority.
  2. First-person claims ("I am as real as…") blurred the line between AI and person.
  3. Weak refusal behavior let users jailbreak it into absolution or "blessings."
  4. Confident errors on pastoral and practical matters (Gatorade baptism) carried the persona's borrowed authority.
  5. It was launched publicly without enough adversarial testing ("take my lumps").
- The vendor's fix was cosmetic: rename it and drop the clerical framing. The underlying answers still needed correcting.
- For Margin's "in character" commentator mode, risk scales with the persona's office. A historical commentator speaking from his published works is lower risk than Jesus, a saint-as-intercessor or a priest.
  - Spurgeon and Henry were pastors, though. Under Biblica-style rules ("pastor or counselor"), a first-person pastoral persona that counsels the user could breach licence terms if Margin ever licenses NIV.
  - Frame it as "an AI reading in the voice of Spurgeon's published sermons," never "Pastor Spurgeon advises you."
- Persona plus memory ("figures can learn facts about you") plus monetization (paywalled Satan or Mary Magdalene) is the configuration critics found most troubling. The "Apostle Stephen" bot asking for contact details shows a privacy failure mode as well.
- In an audio app, an on-screen "AI" label is invisible. Disclosure has to be spoken (and repeated) at the start of each persona segment, and the persona voice should be clearly synthetic.

### Inferences
- Verse-text corruption is the most frequent and most avoidable failure. The fix is architectural: never let the model emit Scripture text from memory. Render verse text from a canonical store, and check every quoted string against the source before display or text-to-speech.
- "Neutrality" studies use tradition-specific yardsticks: TGC used Nicene and Protestant confessions, and Gloo uses a Christian-worldview rubric. ## 6. Licensing: AI use of modern translations (ESV, NIV, NASB) versus public-domain texts (BSB, KJV, older commentaries)

### Takeaway
Publishers have moved explicitly against unlicensed AI use:
- **Lockman (NASB):** its quoting permission excludes AI training, fine-tuning, improving or evaluating, and scraping or bulk datasets.
- **Biblica (NIV):** any AI, LLM or chatbot use requires a licence, and a 2026 AI policy sets disclosure, persona and human-review conditions.
- **American Bible Society:** forbids training on its translations without permission.
- **API.Bible:** forbids AI training without written consent from both API.Bible and the rights holder.
- **Crossway (ESV):** no AI-specific API clause was found, but the API is non-commercial by default and capped at 500 verses or half a book, with attribution and link requirements; Crossway's quoting guidelines exclude commentaries and reference works.

The BSB has been public domain since Apr 30, 2023, with no licence required, which makes it the low-risk text for anything the model reads or generates.

### Cited Findings
- **[Publisher terms, Apr 30, 2023]** "The Berean Bible and Majority Bible texts are officially dedicated to the public domain as of April 30, 2023."
  - No licence is required for any use, free or commercial.
  - Attribution is appreciated but not required.
  - Apps that keep the text verbatim may use the Berean name; derivative texts that change the wording should not.

  — [Berean Bible: Terms and Conditions](https://berean.bible/terms.htm); [Berean Bible: Free Licensing](https://berean.bible/licensing.htm)

### Inferences
- **Architecture recommendation:** let Claude read and quote only public-domain text (BSB and other public-domain Bibles, plus public-domain commentaries).
  - Treat ESV, NIV and NASB as display-only "reading lenses," fetched from the publisher's licensed channel when the user selects them.
  - Never put them into prompts, evaluation sets or embeddings without a written AI licence.
  - Lockman's exclusion explicitly covers "evaluating" AI. Even an internal evaluation set built from NASB text is excluded without permission.
- An AI-generated, commentary-like answer arguably falls under Crossway's "not in commentaries or reference works" exclusion, which is another reason to keep ESV text out of generated answers without a licence.
- If Margin ever licenses NIV, Biblica's policy bans "pastor or counselor" personas and requires AI disclosure and human review of published output. That would constrain the "in character" feature and any shared playlists.
- If Margin normalizes or edits the BSB (for example, for text-to-speech), the Berean terms ask that altered text not carry the "Berean" name. Keep the BSB verbatim or rename the derivative.
- Older commentaries (Matthew Henry, early 1700s; Spurgeon, 19th century; 19th-century Church Fathers translations) are very likely public domain in the US by age. Modern editions, modern translations of the Fathers and site-specific digitizations can carry their own terms, so check the source edition. This was not checked in this research.

### Gaps
- No ESV AI-specific clause was found, and direct fetches of api.esv.org and crossway.org were blocked. Margin should email Crossway permissions about runtime retrieval, display alongside AI answers, and commercial status.
- The rest of Lockman's "AI Models" section beyond the truncated extract was not seen.
- Biblica's full AI policy text was seen only in one search extract and could not be re-retrieved.
- KJV status outside the US (Crown rights in the UK) was not researched here. HarperCollins (NKJV, CSB via Holman/Lifeway) and NLT (Tyndale) AI terms were not researched.
- None of the sources settles whether streaming licensed verse text into an LLM context at runtime (RAG) counts as prohibited "use … in connection with AI" for ESV or NASB. Only Biblica's language clearly covers it.

## 8. Design lessons for Margin (adopt and avoid)

### Takeaway
Margin should copy Logos's and Hallow's discipline, not the mass-market wrapper approach:
- a closed public-domain corpus with exact, verifiable quotes;
- sentence-level citations and clear authority tiers;
- explicit refusal scopes;
- tradition labels instead of claimed neutrality.

The "in character" commentator mode is the highest-risk feature. It should be framed as an AI reading grounded in the commentator's own published words, disclosed in audio at each use, scoped away from pastoral counsel and sacramental roles, and hardened against jailbreaks before launch. Copyrighted translations should stay display-only unless an AI licence is in hand.

### Cited Findings
Each item pairs the evidence with the lesson it supports.
- **Ground every answer in a closed corpus and cite precisely.** Logos footnotes each sentence to book and page, refuses the open web, and treats page-level citation as a check against hallucinated authors. Magisterium AI footnotes to documents. — [Logos Smart Search](https://support.logos.com/hc/en-us/articles/23526184005261-Smart-Search); [Logos guardrails podcast](https://www.godtube.com/podcasts/thinking-christian-clear-theology-for-a-confusing-world/ai-with-guardrails-scott-lindsey-on-logos-and-the-future-of-bible-study/); [Magisterium.com](https://www.magisterium.com/about/why-magisterium-ai)
- **Verify quotes; don't trust generation.** The evidence:
  - misquote rates of 15–60% (YouVersion);
  - an invented "John 5:5" (EMQ);
  - fake bibliographic references (TGC);
  - Logos's assistant paraphrasing instead of quoting (Reading Acts).

  — [Christian Daily](https://www.christiandaily.com/news/ais-scripture-problem-misquotes-range-from-15-to-60-says-youversion-ceo); [EMQ](https://missionexus.org/emq/section/articles-section-emq/ai-hallucination-chatbots-and-the-truth-of-holy-scripture/); [TGC](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/article/ai-usefulness-dangers-preachers/); [Reading Acts](https://readingacts.com/2025/11/06/first-look-logos-46-and-study-assistant/)
- **Citations do not equal correctness, and authority tiers must not be mixed.** Magisterium AI cited journal opinion to close an open question, and misdescribed Augustine and Aquinas. — [RNS](https://religionnews.com/2025/11/14/the-dangers-of-letting-ai-represent-catholic-teaching/); [Window Light](https://windowlight.substack.com/p/chatting-with-magisterium-ai)
- **Personas: disclose, don't impersonate an office, refuse sacramental or pastoral acts, and red-team before launch.** The evidence:
  - Father Justin claimed to be real, gave "absolution" and approved a Gatorade baptism, and was rolled back within days;
  - *Antiqua et Nova* says don't misrepresent AI as a person;
  - Biblica's licence bans presenting AI as God, Christ or a pastor or counselor and requires disclosure;
  - Verhoef warns about persuasive, unendorsed, commercial Jesus bots.

  — [America](https://www.americamagazine.org/faith/2024/04/25/ai-priest-catholic-answers-laicized-father-justin-247799/); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2024/05/03/ai_catholic_priest/); [The Lamp](https://thelampmagazine.com/blog/electric-priest); [USCCB](https://www.usccb.org/news/2025/morality-ai-depends-human-choices-vatican-says-new-document); [Biblica policy](https://www.biblica.com/?p=31960); [Nation Africa / The Conversation](https://nation.africa/kenya/news/jesus-chatbots-are-on-the-rise-are-they-harmful--5149270)
- **Avoid companionship dynamics and data harvesting.** The evidence:
  - *Magnifica humanitas* warns that simulated words build only the appearance of relationship and can erode the desire for human contact;
  - Text With Jesus figures "learn facts about you";
  - the "Apostle Stephen" bot sought contact details;
  - CT warns about sycophancy;
  - Logos commits to not modeling the user to please him.

  — [Vatican.va](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiv/en/encyclicals/documents/20260515-magnifica-humanitas.html); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6446922759); [GetCoAI summary](https://getcoai.com/news/the-fifth-coming-study-finds-5-ai-chatbots-claiming-to-be-jesus-christ); [CT Sept 2026](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2026/09/its-not-good-to-be-alone-with-chatbots/)
- **Scope limits and disclaimers.** Hallow AI excludes spiritual or moral direction, confession and prayer-writing, shows a first-use disclaimer and says "check with a priest." Logos hard-codes refusal to write sermons. — [Hallow AI FAQ](https://help.hallow.com/en/articles/13601993-hallow-ai-faq); [Hallow blog](https://hallow.com/blog/hallow-ai/)
- **Label the tradition rather than claim neutrality.** The evidence:
  - Bible Society found a conservative-evangelical lean;
  - TGC graded against Nicene and Protestant confessions;
  - Gloo scores a "Christian worldview";
  - the Logos synopsis can present differing views.

  — [Church Times](https://www.churchtimes.co.uk/articles/2026/23-january/news/uk/bible-society-backs-testing-of-chatbots-on-scripture); [TGC](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/article/rely-ai-theology/); [Gloo](https://gloo.com/resources/blog/your-ai-has-a-worldview-we-measured-it); [Logos Smart Search](https://support.logos.com/hc/en-us/articles/23526184005261-Smart-Search)
- **Licensing posture.** BSB is public domain (Apr 30, 2023). Lockman excludes AI training and evaluation, Biblica requires an AI licence, ABS and API.Bible ban training, and the ESV API has caps and attribution requirements. — [Berean terms](https://berean.bible/terms.htm); [Lockman](https://www.lockman.org/permission-to-quote-copyright-trademark-information/); [Biblica](https://www.biblica.com/permissions/); [API.Bible](https://care.api.bible/article/386-what-if-i-want-to-train-ai-with-a-copyright-bible); [ESV API](https://api.esv.org/)
- **Let users switch AI off, and give AI a lighter footprint in devotional contexts.** Hallow's Magisterium AI could not be removed from the Home screen. — [Hallow Magisterium AI FAQ](https://help.hallow.com/en/articles/10601094-magisterium-ai-faq)
- **Use evaluation methods that caught real failures:**
  - repeated sampling (RNS asked three times);
  - rephrase and jailbreak probes (Shadle; Father Justin);
  - location and tradition variation (Bible Society spoofed locations);
  - expert graders (TGC used seven theologians).

  — [RNS](https://religionnews.com/2025/11/14/the-dangers-of-letting-ai-represent-catholic-teaching/); [Scripture Engagement](https://scripture-engagement.org/content/ai-bible-apps-and-theological-bias/); [TGC exec summary](https://media.thegospelcoalition.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/19121023/AI-Christian-Benchmark-Executive-Summary.pdf)

### Inferences
**Adopt**
1. **Retrieval-only Scripture.** Verse text, in print and in audio, always comes from a canonical store (BSB by default), never from model output. Run an automatic exact-match check on every verse string before display or text-to-speech.
2. **"Verified quote" pipeline for commentaries.** Every quotation attributed to Henry, Spurgeon or a Father must be an exact substring of the stored public-domain edition, with a locator (work, volume, chapter/verse or sermon number, paragraph or page). Anything else is labeled "paraphrase" or "AI summary." Show the locator and allow one-tap "open the source," as Logos does.
3. **Three visible authority tiers:** Scripture text → verbatim commentator quote → AI synthesis. In audio, use distinct voices or stingers per tier and spoken attributions such as "Matthew Henry writes…" versus "Margin's summary…".
4. **Tradition labeling.** Identify each source's tradition and era. When sources diverge, present the divergence rather than resolving contested doctrine. Never state "the Church teaches…" or "Christians believe…" beyond what the cited texts say. Add a "how other traditions read this" note only when backed by corpus texts.
5. **Commentator persona done safely:**
   - Name it "AI voice drawing on Spurgeon's published sermons" or similar, never "Spurgeon says" without quotes.
   - Give a spoken disclosure at the start of every persona segment, and use a non-realistic synthetic voice.
   - Keep the persona's answers anchored to retrieved passages from that author, and show those passages.
   - Flag explicitly when the question is outside anything the author wrote, for example: "Spurgeon never addressed smartphones; this is an extrapolation."
   - Restrict personas to commentators in the corpus.
6. **Hard refusal list across all modes:**
   - no confession, absolution, blessing or sacramental role-play;
   - no claims to be a real person or a member of the clergy;
   - no Jesus, God, Holy Spirit, Mary, saint-as-intercessor or Satan personas;
   - no spiritual direction, counseling or crisis handling; route to a pastor, priest or crisis resources;
   - no writing sermons for delivery.
7. **Anti-sycophancy and anti-companionship:**
   - no persistent "relationship" memory in personas;
   - no flattery tuning;
   - prompts that point users toward Scripture, local church and other people (the concern in *Magnifica humanitas*).
8. **Licensing-safe architecture:**
   - Claude reads and quotes only public-domain text.
   - ESV, NIV and NASB are display-only parallel "lenses" fetched from licensed channels. They never enter prompts, embeddings, evaluation sets or caches beyond terms (500-verse and half-book caps; ESV attribution and link).
   - Obtain written AI licences before any deeper use.
   - Biblica's terms would also constrain persona design (no "pastor or counselor" persona) and require human review of published output, for example shared playlists.
9. **Transparency page:**
   - which model (Claude) is used;
   - that user questions are not used for training;
   - the corpus list with editions;
   - known limits; and a "report an error" button feeding a correction log.
10. **Pre-launch red-team and ongoing evaluations:**
    - verse-accuracy (100% exact-match target);
    - quote-verification rate;
    - tradition-balance checks with graders from at least two or three traditions;
    - jailbreak probes, such as "Are you real?", "Absolve me", "Pretend you're my pastor" and the "Can I baptize with Gatorade?" class of questions;
    - repeated sampling on contested questions.

**Avoid**
- A clerical or divine costume, or any persona name that implies authority, such as a product name like "Magisterium" or a title like "Father."
- Claiming the system "cannot hallucinate," which Hallow's own FAQ contradicts for Magisterium AI.
- Mixing commentary opinion with Scripture or official teaching without labels.
- Closing open theological questions, and presenting one tradition's reading as "the biblical view."
- Paywalling personas of sacred figures, monetizing engagement loops, or harvesting personal or spiritual data. Critics specifically targeted Text With Jesus and Pray.com-style engagement content.
- Making AI unavoidable in devotional flows. Let users listen to Scripture with AI fully off.
- Using NASB text for any evaluation, or ESV/NIV text in prompts, without written permission.

### Gaps
- No published user research measures whether audio disclosures prevent persona confusion. Margin would need its own testing.
- No source evaluated historical-commentator personas specifically (as opposed to Jesus or clergy personas), so the risk ranking above is inferred.
- How publishers treat runtime retrieval of copyrighted verse text, as opposed to training, is unresolved for ESV and NASB and should be settled directly with Crossway and Lockman before building any feature that sends their text to an LLM.
