# Heir: Bible memory courses, research and options

A plan for themed Bible memory courses that sit beside the catechisms and creeds. Nothing here is built yet.
The verse lists are drafts for review.

## 1. What already exists

Reputable, widely used plans, and what each suggests for Heir:

| Plan | Who | Shape | What to borrow |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Beginning with Christ** | The Navigators | 5 verses, one per assurance: salvation (1 John 5:11–12), answered prayer (John 16:24), victory (1 Corinthians 10:13), forgiveness (1 John 1:9), guidance (Proverbs 3:5–6) | The first five verses of the beginner course. |
| **Topical Memory System** | The Navigators | 60 verses: 5 series, 6 topics each, 2 verses per topic. Series include Living the New Life, Proclaiming Christ, Relying on God's Resources and Being Christ's Disciple | Two verses per topic is a good unit size. Its themes map to courses 2, 9 and 10 below. |
| **Fighter Verses** | Truth78 (Children Desiring God) | A five-year cycle, 52 verses a set (one a week), each tagged with a topic such as Battling Sin, Trusting God, Salvation or Security in God; a shorter Foundation set for ages 3–5 | Verses chosen to fight unbelief and sin: course 7. A small children's set alongside the adult one. |
| **Scripture Memory Fellowship** | SMF (from the Bible Memory Association) | Illustrated themed books, such as *Knowing God* | A course on God's attributes: course 4. |
| **Passages, not just verses** | Dallas Willard (*Renewing the Christian Mind*) | The Ten Commandments, the Lord's Prayer, the Sermon on the Mount, Romans 8, Colossians 3, Philippians 2–4 | Passage courses: 17–20. |
| **Ten Passages to Memorize Cold** | David Mathis, Desiring God | Includes Romans 8:28–39, Psalm 46 and Colossians 1:15–20 | Anchor passages for courses 3, 5 and 6. |
| **Whole chapters and books** | Andrew Davis, *How to Memorize Scripture for Life* (Crossway, 2024) | Long passages by daily repetition, with an Ephesians plan | A later "whole book" track. |
| **First-verses lists** | Many (Scott Slayton, Crosswalk, Christianity.com, Focus on the Family) | 15–30 verses for new believers or children | They agree on a common core: Genesis 1:1, John 3:16, Romans 3:23 and 6:23, Ephesians 2:8–9, Psalm 23, the Lord's Prayer. |

None of these is a standard canon. They agree on the core and differ at the edges. Heir's courses should be its
own selections, informed by these, without copying any one compilation or using its name.

## 2. How big a course should be

Measured against the Westminster Shorter Catechism, whose answers average 31 words:

| Catechism answers | Words |
|---|---|
| 10 | about 200 |
| 15 | about 320 |
| 30 | about 810 |

A Bible verse averages about 25 words, so:

- **Beginner course:** about 15 catechism answers, 300–550 words, 25–30 verses.
- **Themed course:** 15–30 answers, 450–950 words, 25–50 verses.
- **Passage course:** a chapter or a set of psalms, 450–1,000 words. A very long passage (the Sermon on the Mount, about 80 answers) becomes a course with three Parts.

The sizes below were measured on the KJV; the NASB runs about the same.

## 4. How a Bible course would work in Heir

- **Same engine as the catechisms.** Each text is an item. The cue is its reference, or a theme line for a
  child ("God's promise when you're afraid"). The answer is the verse, split into pieces for each track.
- **Units of five texts,** with a lock-in at the end of each unit, as for the catechisms.
- **Parts with their own window.** Courses split into two or three Parts (for example, the Gospel: "The bad news",
  "What Christ did", "New life"), each lighting its own stained-glass window.
- **Both directions** for Emerald: reference to verse, and verse to reference.
- **A meaning question** for each text, like the catechism's "What it means".
- **Overlap counts once.** A verse already learned in one course shows as learned in every course that holds it.
- **A children's version** of each course uses the shorter texts, or the first verse of each passage.
- **Custom courses later:** pick any verses, or start from a course and edit it.

## Starter courses (revised)

The first window has to be reachable in a few weeks, even at five minutes a day, three or four days a week.
So the two starter courses are short. A 5-minute sitting learns about one verse of up to 30 words; longer texts
split into pieces of about 30 words. Add one lock-in sitting and about one review sitting for every three new
pieces.

**First Verses** (replaces the 16-text draft above): 6 verses, 113 words, 9 sittings. About 1½ weeks daily,
2½ weeks at 3–4 days a week. Easiest first, so the first verse is learned in the first sitting.
Genesis 1:1 · Psalm 23:1 · John 3:16 · Romans 6:23 · Proverbs 3:5–6 · Psalm 119:11.
Window options: Christ holding a child (Saint Joseph, Macon, Georgia, CC0); Jesus and the children (Sacred Heart,
New Philadelphia, Ohio, CC0); Jesus, Friend of Children (Saint Louis, Batesville, Indiana, CC BY 4.0).
Encouragement: a goal before starting; "your first Bible verse, by heart" after Genesis 1:1; the morning recall
("still there after a night's sleep"); "say it to someone today"; halfway; a warm welcome back after missed days
with no broken streaks; the finish with Psalm 119:11.

**Christmas** (replaces the 15-text draft above), for Advent (Advent Sunday 2026 is 29 November). No songs.
- A, recommended: Isaiah 9:6 · Matthew 1:21 · John 1:14 · Luke 2:8–14. 238 words, 13 sittings, about 2 weeks
  daily or 3½ weeks at 3–4 days a week. One text per Advent week, ending with Luke 2 said together on Christmas Eve.
- B, quicker: Isaiah 9:6 · Matthew 1:21 · Luke 2:10–11 · Luke 2:14 · John 1:14 · 2 Corinthians 9:15. 161 words,
  12 sittings.
Window: the Nativity, Sacred Heart Church, New Philadelphia, Ohio (CC0), cropped wide.

## 6. The decision that matters most: translation and licensing

To memorize a verse word for word, the app has to hold its exact text on the phone, offline, every day. That's
different from the verse screen, which shows one verse fetched live.

- **NASB 2020 through YouVersion:** your key can read it. Two things need confirming before a whole course stores
  NASB text on the phone:
  - YouVersion's rules on caching and keeping text, which I couldn't read from here.
  - The Lockman Foundation's own quotation limits. Publishers usually cap how many verses an app may hold without
    a written license. The catalog holds 628 verses, and the Sermon on the Mount course alone holds 111.

  The safe path is to email the Lockman Foundation (lockman.org) about a free family memorization app, and to
  check the YouVersion Platform terms on caching.
- **Public domain (BSB or KJV)** can ship today with no limits. The BSB reads in modern English.
- **A per-family choice later:** each family picks a translation, and the app fetches and keeps that version's
  text. That needs the licensing question settled first.

## 7. Suggested order

1. **First Verses**, **Fear, Worry and Peace** and **Christmas**, then **The Gospel** and **Psalms to Know by Heart**. These cover
   beginners, the core of the faith, the bedtime theme and a passage course.
2. The rest of "The faith" and "For the battles of life".
3. Growing in Christ, then Romans 8, Isaiah 53 and the Sermon on the Mount.
4. Custom courses.
