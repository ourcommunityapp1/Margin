# Scripture-memory SERP research

Date run: 2026-11-23. Method: about 45 web searches (standard mode) on generic, non-branded queries. No search-volume data was available, so nothing here is a volume or difficulty estimate.

## Method and limits (read first)

- The search tool is US-only and returns an ordered list of results, but that order is not guaranteed to match Google's live ranking, and it does not show featured snippets, People Also Ask, video carousels or personalization. Treat "top results" as "results the engine surfaced first", not as measured rank.
- Page fetching failed in this environment (DNS refused for biblememory.com, remem.me, biblegateway.com, navigators.org, equip.sbts.edu). Every claim below about what a page says comes from the search tool's result titles, URLs and its short summaries of the results, not from reading the pages. Page types are inferred from the title, domain and URL. Verify any specific claim by opening the page before acting on it.
- Per instructions, no person names are used. Where a source attributes a claim to a named researcher, it is described generically.
- Today is 2026-11-23, so Advent and Christmas content is in season now. Lists dated "2026" will go stale in about five weeks.

## The 8 most useful findings

1. **Review is the one point all how-to pages agree on, but almost none explain why.** Nearly every how-to result (BibleGateway, Desiring God, Crossway, SBTS Equip, Logos, biblememory.com) says to review, and some give schedules (for example "after two days, then weekly for about two months, then monthly", or "reciting once a day for about 100 days"). These come from personal practice, not cited studies. The reasoning is "if you don't use it you lose it".
2. **Research citations are rare, and where they appear they sit on app-vendor pages and are loosely used.** The search tool flagged that vendor pages quote a specific percentage improvement attributed to a 2006 spacing meta-analysis, and a "forget about half within an hour" figure, without a clean primary citation. One Christianity Today piece cites a retrieval-practice study in passing. Several other pages state statistics with no source (the tool flagged these as unreliable). A page that cites primary sources and states the effect honestly would stand out. I did not verify the primary sources in this run.
3. **Tool queries are owned by app stores and app vendors.** "Bible memory app", "best Bible memory apps", "scripture memory app free" and "Scripture Typer" return mostly apps.apple.com, play.google.com and the vendors' own sites. The "best of" comparison pages that appear are vendor-affiliated (for example doc.remem.me/best-bible-memory-apps/ ranks the vendor's own app first). An independent, disclosed comparison is missing.
4. **A few vendor domains also hold the informational ranking slots.** biblememory.com (the maker of The Bible Memory App, formerly Scripture Typer) and remem.me (maker of Remember Me) rank for how-to, spaced-repetition, topical-verse (anxiety, Christmas, Easter) and plan queries, not just for app queries. They have built topical verse libraries.
5. **The how-to head terms are crowded with similar "tips" lists.** For "how to memorize scripture" and "how to memorize Bible verses fast" the same set recurs: biblegateway.com, desiringgod.org, crossway.org, crosswalk.com, logos.com, lwf.org, biblehub.com, amazingfacts.org, goodreads.com author-blog syndication. Angles are nearly identical: start small, understand first, short daily sessions, write it out, partner, pray. Differentiation will come from a concrete plan and explained mechanism, not from another tips list.
6. **Passage-specific queries are weakly served for method.** "memorize Psalm 23" returns devotionals and verse-by-verse tool pages. "memorize Romans 8" returns Bible-text pages, flashcard decks (brainscape) and one-line mentions, with no dedicated how-to-memorize page in the results. "memorize the Ten Commandments" and "memorize the Lord's Prayer" return mixed devotional or tip pages, some good (hand motions, grouping) but no plan with review. "memorize Apostles' Creed" returned almost no method content at all.
7. **Catechism memorization is served by ministry and publisher content, and the tools are quiz-only.** Results come from CPH, Ligonier, TGC, CRCNA, Christianity Today. The catechism apps surfaced (Westminster Shorter, Heidelberg, catechizeMe, New City Catechism, catechism.app) are described as flashcard, quiz and mastery-tracking apps, with no confirmed scheduled review in the listings. I found no page that combines Scripture memory and catechism memory in one plan with review.
8. **Plan and "for the year" queries are fragmented and dated.** The 52-week results are a paywalled commentary page, YouVersion reading plans, biblememory.com group pages, a paid creator product and church PDFs. 2026-specific lists dominate "verses for the year", and 2027 lists were not yet visible on 2026-11-23, so a new-year plan published in December has timing room.

## What angles top pages take

- **Tips lists (5, 7, 10, 27 ways).** Dominant for generic how-to. Advice: start small, understand first, daily short sessions, write it out, say it aloud, memorize with a partner, pray, use cards or an app.
- **Personal-method narratives.** "How I memorized 45 books", "500 verses in a year", "Romans on Medium". Credible as testimony, with no controlled evidence.
- **Why-memorize persuasion.** Desiring God, SBTS Equip, Ligonier and similar. Theology and motivation, with light method.
- **Verse libraries and topic hubs.** biblememory.com, remem.me, scripturememory.com host topic pages (anxiety, Christmas, Easter) that double as app funnels.
- **Kids: games, songs, motions.** Children's-ministry sites dominate with activity lists. Parent involvement is the only repeated structural advice.
- **Programs.** Navigators TMS (60 verses, positioned as a 30-week program per the vendor snippet), Charlotte Mason system, Awana-style apps, ministry plans. These are products, not neutral guides.

## What pages claim about memory, and whether they cite research

- Common practitioner claims: "repetition over time", "review or lose it", "understanding before memorizing", "multi-sensory (write, say, hear) helps", "small chunks". None of these are linked to primary studies on the pages the tool summarized.
- Specific unsourced or loosely sourced figures seen in results: a "100 days" daily-recitation rule of thumb; "after two days, then weekly for about two months, then monthly"; a vendor's "forget about 50% within an hour"; a vendor's "200% improvement" tied to a 2006 spacing meta-analysis; several statistics flagged by the tool as unsourced. I did not verify any of these. Do not repeat them without checking the primary papers.
- Where research is mentioned at all: app-vendor educational pages (spacing, forgetting curve), one Christianity Today illustration (retrieval practice), a seminary thesis with a research section (not read), and the 2026 Biola interview noted above. A 2024 peer-reviewed meta-analysis on forgetting-curve shapes also exists (PMC11680664), but it was not Scripture-specific and was not read in full.
- No result gave Scripture-specific controlled evidence. Any new page should say plainly that it is applying general memory science to Scripture.

## Where pages are thin, outdated, or app-store-only

- **App-store-only:** "Bible verse memorization app for kids" and "scripture memory app free" return almost only store listings. "catechism memory app" does the same.
- **Vendor-owned comparisons:** "best Bible memory apps" is answered by a vendor's own comparison plus a general blog. No disclosed, criteria-based comparison.
- **Outdated:** many tips pages are old (a 2013 BibleGateway post, a 2004 catechism article, a 2013 Christianity Today piece, a 2017 Crossway Easter list). Lists branded "2026" will date within weeks.
- **Paywalled or partial:** the LifeWay 52-week plan is partly paywalled; the tool saw only intro text.
- **Low-quality or mirrored:** scribd.com uploads, goodreads.com author-blog syndication, and several mirror pages on unrelated hostnames appear for generic queries, which suggests the real quality bar is low.
- **Thin on method:** Romans 8, Apostles' Creed, Lord's Prayer, Ten Commandments (no plan with review), Catholic-specific how-to.

## Queries that look underserved

(Based on what the results offered, not on volume.)

1. "memorize Romans 8" and other whole-chapter passage queries: results are Bible text and flashcards.
2. "memorize the Apostles' Creed" and creed or catechism memorization with a schedule.
3. "memorize the Ten Commandments" and "memorize the Lord's Prayer" as plan-plus-review pages (existing pages are tips or devotionals).
4. "I memorized verses and forgot them" and "review plan for memorized verses": answered by practitioner posts with differing schedules and no explanation of mechanism.
5. "scripture memory for adults" beyond the Navigators and publisher samples, especially a plan that fits adult schedules.
6. "Christmas verses to memorize" and "Easter verses to memorize" as dated, day-by-day plans (existing pages are lists), with Advent starting soon and Easter 2027 later in the spring (confirm the date).
7. "ESV vs NASB for memorization": answered only by general translation-choice posts. No page tied to review and long-term retention, and NASB has no home page of its own for memory.
8. "scripture memory plan 52 weeks" free and complete with review built in.
9. Catechism plus Scripture in one weekly plan, for Westminster, Heidelberg and similar.
10. Independent, disclosed comparison of apps against a stated criterion (does it actually schedule review, does it let you add your own passage or catechism).

## What a research-plus-plan page could add

Content to build (design suggestions, not findings):

- **A short, honest explanation of why memorized text fades and what slows it.** Cover recall after weeks away, the spacing effect and retrieval practice, in plain language with links to primary sources checked by us before publishing. Label what is general memory science versus what is tradition or practitioner habit. Avoid unsourced percentages.
- **A single clear plan.** For example: learn a small unit, recall without looking, then review at widening gaps, and drop back a step after a miss. State the interval pattern as a recommendation with a rationale, and note that sources vary on exact intervals.
- **A "returning after a break" path.** Few pages address picking back up after weeks away; it matches a real search intent ("I forgot what I memorized").
- **Passage-ready schedules.** Psalm 23, Romans 8, Ten Commandments, Lord's Prayer, Apostles' Creed, a catechism section: each as a dated plan with a review calendar. This also covers the thin passage queries.
- **A printable or on-page tracker** that works without an app, and an honest, criteria-based note on when an app helps (the app-store-only gap).
- **Translation-consistency guidance.** One translation for memorization is the repeated advice, so say it, and explain licensing for printing verses (NASB and ESV have usage terms; check before reproducing text in cards).
- **Seasonal plans published ahead of the season.** A Christmas plan now; an Easter plan before Lent; a new-year 52-week plan in December.
- **Family and kids versions** that explain the same review idea at a child's level, since kids pages are mostly activity lists.
- **Catechism companion.** Pair a catechism question each week with a Scripture passage, with the same review rule.

## Cautions

- Everything above is based on search-tool summaries without opening the pages. Before writing, open the 5 to 8 pages that will be direct competitors for each target query and note the actual headings, length, date and citations.
- Vendor pages (biblememory.com, remem.me) are strong on the informational slots but have an obvious product interest. A neutral page needs to disclose its own context.
- I did not search for or include any person's name or brand beyond generic app and publisher names that appear in result titles. No search volumes were available, so prioritize by search-intent fit and the thinness described above, then check Search Console data after launch.
