# Question bank: Bible memorization and catechism study

Purpose: a bank of real, searcher-worded questions to decide which public pages to write and how to word H1/H2 headings for search and AI answer engines.

No search volumes are given anywhere in this file. None were available and none were invented.

## How this was gathered, and its limits

- Tools used: web search only. Page fetching failed for every host tried (puritanboard.com, simplycharlottemason.com, quora.com, desiringgod.org returned DNS/allow-list errors), and reddit.com is blocked from search entirely.
- So "seen" means: the question appeared as a page title, thread title, podcast episode title or article heading in a search result, or was reported in a search-result summary of that page. I did not read full pages or thread bodies. Treat thread bodies as unverified.
- Not reached: Reddit, App Store reviews (only listing text via search), Google/Bing autocomplete, People Also Ask.
- Questions marked INF were inferred by me from the angles in the brief or from patterns in seen material. They are plausible, not observed.
- Wording: where a source title was a statement or a topic, I rewrote it as a typed question. The source column says what was seen.

Source-type codes:

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| QUORA | Quora question title seen in search results |
| FORUM | Forum thread title seen in search results (SCM = homeschool forum; PB = Puritan Board; MV = Memverse forum) |
| BLOG | Blog or article title or heading seen |
| POD | Podcast episode title seen |
| APP | App Store / Play listing or vendor comparison page seen via search |
| SUM | Complaint or question reported in a search-result summary of a forum/blog page (body not read) |
| INF | Inferred, not seen |

## Part 3. Page map

### 3.1 Wording rules suggested by what was seen

1. Lead with the question, in the words people type: "How do you...", "How long does it take to...", "Why can't I...", "What age...", "Is it okay to...". The seen titles overwhelmingly use these forms.
2. Put a one- or two-sentence direct answer immediately under the H1, then the detail. This suits answer engines and featured snippets.
3. Use concrete nouns: "Bible verses", "Westminster Shorter Catechism", "New City Catechism", "Heidelberg Catechism". Searchers name the specific catechism.
4. Use one H2 per sub-question (for example "How long does it take to memorize Psalm 23?"). Seen how-long answers split by passage size.
5. Be honest about evidence. Most seen advice is anecdote or opinion. Label rules of thumb as such, and state sources' disagreement (for example on whether one app uses spaced repetition).
6. Do not repeat the "brain benefits" claim (H9) without a real study.
